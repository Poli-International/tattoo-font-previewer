## What is Tattoo Font Previewer?

Tattoo Font Previewer is a free browser-based tool that renders your text in 107 open source fonts chosen for tattoo lettering. You type a name, date, quote, or phrase once, and every font card in the gallery updates as you type. The tool then lets you adjust how that lettering would actually sit on skin: font size from 12 px to 144 px, letter spacing, line height, and an arc control that bends the text up to +80 or down to -80 for chest rockers and inverted curves.

The gallery is organized by style and by cultural tradition. Style filters cover blackletter / Old English (17 fonts), script / cursive (22), chicano / ornate script (8), fineline / minimalist (14), gothic / dark (13), and decorative (16). Tradition filters cover French, Italian, German, Dutch, Spanish, Portuguese, Japanese (kanji and kana), and Chinese (hanzi calligraphy), 8 fonts each. A font can belong to both a style and a tradition and appear under both.

Beyond previewing, the tool supports stencil preparation. A thermal stencil outline mode converts lettering into hollow outlines for thermal transfer printers and carbon copiers, with a selectable matrix line weight (1 pt, 2 pt, 3 pt) and transfer ink color (thermal purple or carbon blue). A DPI calculator converts the on-screen lettering into printed width and height in centimeters and inches at 203 DPI or 300 DPI, showing the formula it uses. You can pin up to 10 favorites, open an A/B comparison panel that shares size and spacing between both sides, and export any font card as a PNG at up to 7200 x 2400 px. Fonts you upload yourself as .ttf or .otf can also be exported as vector SVG.

Everything runs in your browser. Your text, favorites, and exported images are processed locally and never sent to Poli International.

## How to Use the Tattoo Font Previewer

### Step 1: Enter your preview text
Find the **Preview Text** field at the top of the tool. Click into the text box (placeholder: "Enter your lettering text (e.g., names, dates, quotes)...") and type your lettering. Every font card updates as you type. Click **Clear Text** below the field to empty it.

### Step 2: Set font size and spacing
Drag the **Font Size** slider between 12 px and 144 px, or click a preset: **Small (24 px)**, **Medium (48 px)**, **Large (72 px)**, or **Extra Large (96 px)**. Drag **Letter Spacing** or **Line Height** to adjust the space between characters and between lines. Click **Reset** to return to defaults.

### Step 3: Bend the text with the arc control
Drag **Text Bend (Arc)** to the right to arch the text upward (up to +80) or to the left to curve it downward (up to -80). Or click a preset: **Flat**, **Arc Up** for a chest rocker, or **Arc Down** for an inverted curve.

### Step 4: Choose a background contrast
Find **Background Contrast** below the bend controls. Choose **White** for paper contrast, **Black** to judge negative space, **Transparent Background** for a checkerboard, or **Simulated Skin** for a skin-tone background.

### Step 5: Filter the font gallery
The filter bar sits directly above the gallery, and each filter shows how many fonts it contains. Click **All Fonts** to see all 107. Filter by style, or by cultural tradition. A font can belong to a style and a tradition and appear under both.

### Step 6: Handle Japanese and Chinese characters
When you view Japanese or Chinese fonts, an amber notice appears above the gallery explaining that a character can change meaning depending on context, and that a sentence assembled from dictionaries can read very differently to a native speaker. Click one of the 29 example concepts, such as **Courage (勇)**, **Freedom (自由)**, **Peace (平和)**, **Love (愛)**, or **Gratitude (感謝)**, to see the characters across all fonts. Have meaning and character form confirmed by a native speaker or qualified calligrapher before the stencil touches skin.

### Step 7: Load your own studio font
Open **Load Custom Studio Font** and drag in a .ttf, .otf, or .woff2 file, or click to choose one. Your font appears in the gallery labeled **Studio Custom** and works with all the controls above. The file is read in your browser and never uploaded. Click **Remove Font** to take it out.

### Step 8: Turn on thermal stencil outline mode
Toggle **Thermal Stencil Outline** to convert the lettering into hollow outlines for thermal transfer printers and carbon copiers. Choose a **Stencil Line Weight**: 1 pt for fineline and detail, 2 pt for a standard thermal stencil, or 3 pt for a bold carbon transfer. Choose a **Transfer Ink Color**: thermal purple or carbon blue.

### Step 9: Check physical print dimensions (DPI calculator)
**Physical Print Dimensions (DPI Calculator)** converts the displayed lettering into printed width and height in centimeters and inches, at 203 DPI (standard thermal printer) or 300 DPI (high resolution). The formula it uses is shown: width = pixels / DPI x 2.54 cm.

### Step 10: Compare two fonts side by side
Click **Add to Favorites** on a card to pin it. Pinned fonts appear under **Your Favorite Fonts** above the gallery, up to 10. Click **A/B Compare** to open the comparison panel and choose Font A and Font B. Both sides share size and spacing, so the visible difference is the font alone. Click **Swap Fonts** to switch sides. Click **Copy Name** to copy a font's name, or **Clear Favorites** to empty the list.

### Step 11: Export images
Click **Download PNG** on a font card. Choose an **Export Resolution**: **Standard (2400 x 800 px) - Screen reference**, **High Resolution Print (4800 x 1600 px) - Stencil preparation**, or **Master Resolution (7200 x 2400 px) - High-precision tracing**. Under **Export Background**, choose transparent, white, black, or simulated skin, then click **Download PNG Image**. For a studio font you loaded as .ttf or .otf, **Download Vector SVG** also appears, exporting the lettering as vector paths for cutting plotters, plotters, and vector editors. The 107 catalog fonts export as PNG only.

## Use-Case Examples

### Example 1: Chest rocker in blackletter
A client wants a family name arched across the chest. The artist types the name into the Preview Text field, sets Font Size to **Large (72 px)**, clicks the **Arc Up** preset to bend the text into a rocker shape, and filters the gallery to **Blackletter / Old English**. They pin three candidates with **Add to Favorites**, open **A/B Compare**, and export the winner at **High Resolution Print (4800 x 1600 px)** with a transparent background for the stencil printer.

### Example 2: Fineline script name with simulated skin background
A client wants a delicate script name on the inner forearm. The artist sets **Background Contrast** to **Simulated Skin** to judge how fine strokes read against a skin tone, drags **Letter Spacing** slightly wider to keep letters distinct, and checks **Physical Print Dimensions (DPI Calculator)** at 300 DPI to confirm the lettering stays within the planned width. They export a **Standard (2400 x 800 px)** PNG as a screen reference for the client to approve.

### Example 3: Japanese kanji concept with native-speaker verification
A client wants a single kanji for courage. The artist filters the gallery to **Japanese (kanji and kana)**, clicks the example concept **Courage (勇)** to load the character across all Japanese fonts, and reads the amber notice above the gallery. ## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Can I use these fonts for commercial tattoos?**
The 107 catalog fonts are published on Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License or Apache 2.0, both of which allow commercial use, including tattoos and stencils. For a studio font you load yourself, your own license for that font applies.

**Can I export lettering as a stencil or tracing file?**
Yes. **Download PNG** produces an image up to 7200 x 2400 px, and thermal stencil outline mode gives hollow outlines at the line weight you choose. Transparent and white backgrounds work with thermal stencil printers and tracing layers in apps like Procreate.

**Why does the tool warn about small script lettering?**
Fine lines soften and spread as a tattoo ages. A common guideline among lettering specialists is to keep the x-height of script lettering at 8 to 10 millimeters or more so letters stay distinguishable. Check printed size with the DPI calculator before committing.

**Can I bend lettering to fit a body area?**
Yes. The bend control arches the lettering upward or downward, which helps you judge how a phrase sits on a collarbone, ribs, or as a chest rocker.

**Does the tool save or upload my client's lettering?**
No. The lettering is processed in your browser and never transmitted or stored remotely. Only your favorites, language, and color mode are remembered, in your own browser.

**How many fonts can I pin for comparison?**
Up to 10. Pinned fonts appear under **Your Favorite Fonts** above the gallery, and any two of them can be opened in the A/B comparison panel.

**What file formats can I upload for a custom studio font?**
.ttf, .otf, or .woff2. Uploaded fonts are labeled **Studio Custom** in the gallery and can be exported as vector SVG, which the 107 catalog fonts cannot.

**What is the difference between the three export resolutions?**
Standard (2400 x 800 px) is a screen reference, High Resolution Print (4800 x 1600 px) is for stencil preparation, and Master Resolution (7200 x 2400 px) is for high-precision tracing.

**What DPI options does the print size calculator use?**
203 DPI for a standard thermal printer and 300 DPI for high resolution. The formula shown is width = pixels / DPI x 2.54 cm.

**Which languages does the interface support?**
English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, and Dutch. The Documentation tab follows the same language as the tool.
